# Nigeria JSS 1 — Basic Science

**Instructions:** Select the best answer. The answer guide and explanations are after the questions.

## Answer guide and explanations

1. **A** — Science uses observation and evidence to investigate questions.
2. **B** — A ruler measures short lengths such as a pencil.
3. **B** — Reading instructions and using appropriate protective equipment reduce risk.
4. **A** — Goggles protect the eyes from splashes and particles.
5. **B** — Alert the teacher and rinse with plenty of clean water; do not rub the eye.
6. **C** — Running can cause collisions, spills, and injuries.
7. **C** — A growing bean plant carries out life processes.
8. **B** — Living organisms need energy, although they obtain it in different ways.
9. **B** — Reproduction is the production of offspring.
10. **A** — Respiration releases usable energy from food in cells.
11. **A** — Bending towards light is a response to a stimulus.
12. **A** — Roots commonly anchor plants and absorb water and mineral salts.
13. **B** — Leaves contain chlorophyll and are the main site of photosynthesis in most plants.
14. **A** — After fertilisation, the ovary develops into a fruit in flowering plants.
15. **A** — The young root, or radicle, is usually the first part to emerge.
16. **A** — Most seeds need water, oxygen, and a suitable temperature to germinate.
17. **C** — A goat is a mammal; it has hair and feeds its young with milk.
18. **A** — Insects have three pairs of legs, giving six in total.
19. **A** — Fish use gills to take dissolved oxygen from water.
20. **A** — Vertebrates are animals with a backbone.
21. **A** — The heart pumps blood through blood vessels.
22. **A** — The lungs exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide with the air.
23. **A** — The digestive system breaks food down and absorbs nutrients.
24. **A** — The skeleton supports the body and protects organs such as the brain and lungs.
25. **B** — The ears detect sound.
26. **A** — Carbohydrates are a major energy source in the diet.
27. **A** — Protein supplies materials used for growth and tissue repair.
28. **A** — Vitamins are needed in small amounts and support normal body functions.
29. **A** — This meal combines a staple, protein-rich beans, vegetables, and water.
30. **A** — Water supports body functions; inadequate water can cause dehydration.
31. **A** — Handwashing removes many germs and lowers the chance of transferring them.
32. **A** — Covering a cough helps limit droplets reaching other people or surfaces.
33. **A** — Measles can spread between people; the other options are injuries or conditions that are not contagious.
34. **A** — An infected female Anopheles mosquito can transmit the malaria parasite when it bites.
35. **A** — Mosquitoes can breed in standing water, so removing it reduces breeding sites.
36. **A** — The wrong medicine or dose can cause harm; follow qualified medical advice.
37. **A** — Regular brushing with fluoride toothpaste helps prevent tooth decay.
38. **A** — Clean water can rinse dirt from a minor cut; harsh fuels and oils are unsafe.
39. **A** — Get responsible adult and emergency assistance promptly; avoid unsafe movement or treatment.
40. **A** — Beans and eggs contain protein used for growth and repair.
41. **A** — Matter has mass and occupies space.
42. **A** — A solid generally has a fixed shape and volume.
43. **B** — A liquid keeps its volume but takes the shape of its container.
44. **C** — A gas spreads to occupy the available space in its container.
45. **A** — Melting changes solid ice into liquid water.
46. **A** — Evaporation is a surface change from liquid to gas.
47. **A** — During condensation, water vapour cools and becomes liquid droplets.
48. **A** — Melted wax can solidify again when cooled; burning, rusting, and cooking are chemical changes.
49. **A** — Iron is magnetic; wood, glass, and rubber are not attracted in the usual way.
50. **A** — Copper allows electric charge to flow readily and is used in wires.
51. **A** — Plastic is an electrical insulator commonly placed around conducting wire.
52. **A** — Filter paper traps insoluble sand while water passes through.
53. **A** — Water evaporates and leaves dissolved salt behind.
54. **A** — A magnet attracts the iron filings but not the sand.
55. **A** — Banana peel can be broken down by decomposers; the other materials persist much longer.
56. **A** — Recycling can reduce disposal and the need for some new raw materials.
57. **A** — Clear glass transmits most visible light, so objects can be seen through it.
58. **A** — Hardness describes resistance to scratching or indentation.
59. **A** — Never taste unknown substances or smell them directly; follow laboratory safety instructions.
60. **A** — Rusting forms a new substance, iron oxide, so it is a chemical change.
61. **A** — The Sun is a natural source of light for Earth during the day.
62. **A** — Reflection is the bouncing of light from a surface.
63. **A** — Clear glass transmits most light and allows a clear view through it.
64. **A** — A shadow forms where an opaque object blocks light.
65. **A** — Vibrations create sound waves.
66. **A** — Sound can travel through solids; it cannot travel through a vacuum.
67. **A** — Sunlight is continually replenished and is a renewable energy source.
68. **A** — Coal forms over geological time and is not replenished quickly for human use.
69. **A** — A lamp changes electrical energy mainly into light, with some heat.
70. **A** — A speaker changes electrical signals into sound vibrations.
71. **A** — The fan's motor converts electrical energy into movement, and some energy becomes sound and heat.
72. **A** — A ramp is an inclined plane that helps raise or lower objects.
73. **A** — A pulley at a well changes the direction of the pulling force on a bucket.
74. **A** — A seesaw pivots around a fulcrum and acts as a lever.
75. **A** — Simple machines can change force size or direction, helping people do tasks.
76. **A** — Humans use oxygen in respiration.
77. **A** — Nitrogen is about 78% of dry air, making it the largest component.
78. **A** — Plants take in carbon dioxide as a raw material for photosynthesis.
79. **A** — Rain replenishes many rivers, lakes, and groundwater supplies.
80. **A** — Evaporation changes liquid water into water vapour.
81. **A** — Condensation forms liquid droplets as water vapour cools.
82. **A** — Keeping contaminants away protects water quality and health.
83. **A** — Erosion is the wearing away and movement of soil, especially topsoil.
84. **A** — Plant roots hold soil and reduce the impact of flowing rainwater.
85. **A** — Trees provide habitats and their roots help bind soil.
86. **A** — Smoke adds harmful particles and gases to the air.
87. **A** — Reusing suitable containers reduces the amount thrown away.
88. **A** — A habitat is the place where an organism lives and obtains what it needs.
89. **A** — Green plants make their own food and are producers in most food chains.
90. **A** — Fungi and many bacteria decompose dead material and return nutrients to the environment.
91. **A** — Small clay particles hold water between them more strongly than coarse sand does.
92. **A** — Larger spaces between sand grains allow water to drain more quickly.
93. **A** — Compost is decomposed organic matter that can improve soil structure and add nutrients.
94. **A** — A hoe loosens soil and is a common garden tool.
95. **A** — Plants need water for transport and other processes involved in growth.
96. **A** — Compost and suitable manure return organic matter and nutrients to soil.
97. **A** — A thermometer measures temperature, including body temperature when designed for that use.
98. **A** — Changing water amount while comparing similar plants tests how water affects growth.
99. **A** — Repeating helps reveal whether a result is consistent rather than a one-off.
100. **A** — A ruler can measure a plant's height at regular intervals.
